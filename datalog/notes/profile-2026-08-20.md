# The profile — what the engine actually spends, and what the earlier notes got wrong

Third entry in this project's performance record, and the one the ROADMAP's
*Performance* → **Profile the engine** item asked for. It exists to produce one
number — the derivation recorder's share of a run — because
[`ROADMAP.md`](../ROADMAP.md) sequences the pillar-1 decision behind it. It produced
that number, and in getting there it also **falsified the ranking both earlier notes
gave**, including the lead they each put first.

**Snapshot, not a live reference.** Measured **2026-08-20** at `2fa7157`. AMD Ryzen 7
5700G (16 threads, 30 GB), rustc 1.96.1 — the same machine and toolchain as
[`performance-baseline.md`](performance-baseline.md) and
[`cross-engine-benchmark.md`](cross-engine-benchmark.md), so all three files'
numbers are nominally comparable. *Nominally* is doing work in that sentence; see
**The baseline did not reproduce** below.

Corpus: the 26 programs of `~/datalog-cross-engine-bench-2026-08-17/`, regenerated
from its fixed seed, plus a rows × groups aggregation grid and a symbol-width ladder
generated for this session. Scripts, the cut R patch and the raw results are at
**`~/datalog-profile-2026-08-20/`**, outside the repo by the same convention. Builds are `--no-default-features` (the corpus has no
imports, and skipping the bundled DuckDB build makes an A/B cycle 7 s); that build
measures 0–9% faster than the default-features one, consistently, on every shape —
it does not map a 52 MB binary — and the difference cancels inside an A/B pair.

## Method: cuts, not a sampler

Most of what follows is A/B builds, allocation counting and scaling grids — the
right instrument for a ranked hypothesis, the wrong one for discovering an unranked
one. `samply` sampling was added partway through, once
`kernel.perf_event_paranoid` was lowered to 1, and it earned its place: it is the
only measurement here that changed a conclusion the A/B builds had already reached
(see *Value representation*).

Four scratch builds, in a throwaway git worktree, none of them committed:

| build | what is removed |
|---|---|
| **cut A** | the `derivations` map insert in `Model::insert_derived` |
| **cut B** | cut A, plus the per-premise deep tuple clone and the premise vector |
| **cut R** | *added*, not removed: seek the bound prefix instead of scanning (below) |
| **cut RB** | cut R and cut B together |

**Every number here passed an answer guard**: each build's sorted stdout is
byte-identical to stock's on all 26 corpus programs, and each matches the
generator's independently-computed answer count. A timing delta with a changed
digest was never reported.

## The recorder, measured at last

`min`-of-N wall clock, peak RSS from `/usr/bin/time -f %M`.

| shape | stock | cut A | cut B | cut B saves | RSS stock → cut B |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| chain_400 | 0.554 s | 0.422 | 0.391 | 29% | 116 → 34 MB (−71%) |
| chain_800 | 3.545 s | 3.017 | 2.960 | 17% | 458 → 127 MB (−72%) |
| sparse_200 | 0.412 s | 0.278 | 0.247 | 40% | 77 → 19 MB (−75%) |
| sparse_400 | 2.764 s | 2.327 | 2.113 | 24% | 303 → 68 MB (−77%) |
| **sparse_800** | **46.30 s** | 44.25 | 45.36 | **2%** | 1203 → 265 MB (−78%) |
| negation_400 | 1.884 s | 1.618 | 1.458 | 23% | 203 → 46 MB (−77%) |
| agg_50000 | 1.490 s | 1.360 | 1.305 | 12% | 71 → 70 MB (−1%) |
| join_4000 | 0.442 s | 0.432 | 0.457 | −3% | 19 → 17 MB (−8%) |

**The recorder is a memory cost, not a time cost.** 70–78% of peak RSS on every
recursive shape, consistently — and a time share that *falls* as the workload grows,
from 40% at `sparse_200` to **2% at `sparse_800`**. The sibling engine's 13× for its
own lineage semiring does not transfer at any size.

The falling share is the tell, and it points at the real finding: as the relation
grows, an unindexed full-relation scan swamps an `O(log n)` map insert. **The
recorder was never `sparse_800`'s problem.** Cut B still leaves 45 s there.
`performance-baseline.md`'s hypothesis 1 — the recorder as the top suspect for the
35× cliff — is **killed on time and confirmed on memory**.

Allocation counts, from a counting `#[global_allocator]` in a scratch build
(std-only; the crate's zero-dep policy makes a heap-profiler crate the wrong
instrument even here):

| program | build | allocations | bytes | peak live |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| sparse_400 | stock | 8 037 451 | 355 MB | 227 MB |
| sparse_400 | cut B | 3 665 046 | 123 MB | 46 MB |
| chain_400 | stock | 3 176 517 | 149 MB | 88 MB |
| chain_400 | cut B | 1 575 713 | 58 MB | 21 MB |
| agg_r50000_g1600 | stock | 1 924 749 | 111 MB | 53 MB |
| agg_r50000_g1600 | cut B | 1 758 739 | 105 MB | 52 MB |

So the recorder is **54% of allocations, 65% of bytes and 80% of live peak** on
`sparse_400`, and **9% of allocations** on an aggregate. Aggregation owes it nothing.

## The finding neither note has: the relation is already an index, and nothing seeks it

`Tuple` is `Vec<Value>` with a derived `Ord`, so a relation's `BTreeSet` is
lexicographically ordered by column, and **the tuples sharing a bound prefix are a
contiguous range**. `enumerate_from`'s atom arm iterates the whole relation anyway
and rejects non-matching tuples one at a time in `try_match`. `Value::unifies_with`
is `!self.is_absent() && self == other` — exact equality, except that `absent`
matches nothing — so seeking a non-absent bound prefix is *exactly* equivalent, not
an approximation.

**Cut R** is that seek, ~20 lines: collect the leading constants and already-bound
variables, `range(prefix..).take_while(shares prefix)`, fall back to the full scan
when the prefix is empty or contains `absent`.

| shape | stock | cut R | speedup |
|---|---|---|---|
| join_4000 | 0.434 s | 0.034 | **12.7×** |
| sparse_800 | 48.09 s | 4.71 | **10.2×** |
| agg_50000 | 1.399 s | 0.155 | **9.0×** |
| chain_800 | 3.485 s | 1.502 | 2.3× |
| negation_400 | 1.891 s | 0.842 | 2.3× |

It is an **exponent**, not a constant — which is S6's currency:

| log-log slope | stock | cut R |
|---|---|---|
| sparse 400→800 | n^4.16 | **n^2.14** |
| chain 400→800 | n^2.64 | n^2.19 |
| negation 200→400 | n^3.02 | n^2.58 |
| join 2000→4000 | n^2.00 | **n^1.06** |

Closure output on these graphs is itself n², so cut R puts the recursive shapes at
the output bound, and takes a fixpoint-free 3-way join from quadratic to linear.
Peak RSS is unchanged everywhere — same model, same recorder.

**Cut R passes the test suite.** 422 library tests, including the whole property
suite (E1–E6, B\*, C\*, D\*), plus every integration test, with results identical to
stock. It is a prototype, not a patch: the negated-atom and aggregate arms still
scan, join-order selection interacts with it, and it has had no review. But "the fix
is expected to be small once profiled" (ROADMAP) is now measured rather than hoped.

## Aggregation: right mechanism, wrong number

The ROADMAP records "2.5× the rows at a fixed group count costs 9.6×". That is not
reproducible, and the reason is the generator: `gen_agg` ties groups to `n // 50`, so
the corpus ladder moves rows *and* groups together and the whole product was charged
to rows. Varying them independently (output size is `groups` rows, so printing is
held constant down each column):

| rows \ groups | 200 | 400 | 800 | 1600 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 000 | 0.046 | 0.067 | 0.111 | 0.178 |
| 30 000 | 0.154 | 0.235 | 0.438 | 0.581 |
| 50 000 | 0.314 | 0.545 | 1.208 | 1.589 |

5× the rows at fixed groups costs 5.2–10.9×; 8× the groups at fixed rows costs
3.7–5.1×. Cost tracks the **product**, at ~20 ns per tuple visit, with a per-visit
cost that itself grows with relation size — a cache effect, not an algorithmic one.
So the *mechanism* the ROADMAP suspected is confirmed exactly: the aggregate goal
rescans the whole aggregated relation per group.

Under cut R the same grid collapses:

| | 5× the rows | 8× the groups |
|---|---|---|
| stock | 6.8–10.9× | 3.7–5.1× |
| cut R | **5.2–6.2×** | **0.96–1.23×** |

Groups become free and rows become linear: Θ(groups × rows) → Θ(rows). The
aggregation item and the scan are **one item**, not two.

## Value representation: killed as a time lead

Both earlier notes rank interning / `Rc<str>` near the top, on the strength of a
~15 k tuples/s (long Rust identifiers) vs ~270 k/s (`n123`) throughput gap between
them. Holding the graph fixed — the same `chain_400`, only the symbol width varied:

| symbol width | min wall | allocations | bytes | peak live | RSS |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 0.571 s | 3 176 637 | 150 MB | 88 MB | 117 MB |
| 16 | 0.583 s | 3 176 992 | 172 MB | 98 MB | 117 MB |
| 32 | 0.570 s | 3 177 435 | 202 MB | 111 MB | 129 MB |
| 64 | 0.614 s | 3 177 447 | 261 MB | 137 MB | 154 MB |

**16× the symbol length costs 7.5% of wall clock and zero extra allocations**, while
adding 75% to bytes and 32% to RSS. The allocation *count* is flat to four
significant figures, which is what it should be: same graph, same number of
`String`s. So the two notes' throughput gap is **not** symbol length.

**But do not conclude from that row that interning is memory-only** — that is the
inference the A/B build supports and the sampler refutes. Self time on the engine's
own thread, `agg_50000`:

| | share |
|---|---|
| `<ir::Value as PartialEq>::eq` | 30.5% |
| `__memcmp_avx2_movbe` (libc) | ~33% |
| `slice::cmp::eq` | 6.0% |
| `engine::enumerate_from` | 3.7% |
| B-tree node traversal | 2.4% |

**About 70% of that run is comparing values**, and on `sparse_400` it is ~31%. Width
does not matter because `memcmp` on a 4-byte symbol is dominated by the call, not the
bytes — which is exactly the cost an interned `u32` would delete. So interning is a
real *time* lead against **today's** engine.

It stops being one the moment the prefix seek lands, because the seek removes the
comparisons rather than making each cheaper. Re-sampled under cut R, `Value::eq`
falls from 30.5% to **1.9%** on `agg_50000` and from 12.7% to 3.0% on `sparse_400`,
and `memcmp` from ~33% to ~5%. What is left on top is the allocator —
`_int_malloc`, `__libc_free`, `unlink_chunk`, `_int_free_merge_chunk`, ~26% of
`sparse_400` — which is where the recorder's 54% of allocations lands.

So: interning is a memory item **provided the seek goes first**, and a time item if
it does not. The sequence is the finding.

Also corrected: a read of `try_match` suggested it allocates a `Vec<usize>` per
candidate tuple including mismatches. It does not — `Vec::new()` does not allocate,
and the `push` fires only on a newly bound slot. The counts settle it: 1.9 M
allocations across ~80 M tuple visits on `agg_r50000_g1600`.

## The baseline did not reproduce

Rebuilding **`0356b04`** — the exact commit `cross-engine-benchmark.md` measured, on
the machine it measured — and running its own corpus:

| program | note, 2026-08-17 | `0356b04` today | `2fa7157` today |
|---|---|---|---|
| chain_800 | 3.78 s | 3.24 | 3.49 |
| sparse_400 | 2.90 s | 2.57 | 2.70 |
| sparse_800 | **65.14 s** | **43.25** | 46.52 |
| agg_50000 | **3.77 s** | **1.28** | 1.34 |

Peak RSS reproduces to within 0.3% on all four. **Wall clock does not**, and the
error grows with runtime — 1.17× on the shortest, 2.9× on `agg_50000`. The engine
did not change; those two headline numbers are not reproducible.

Two consequences, both load-bearing:

- **"The one shape a sibling engine wins outright" does not hold.** Re-running both
  engines today on the unchanged corpus, with `tsdl` still at `fe8dae2`:
  `agg_50000` is **1.73 s ours against 2.92 s theirs** — we win by 1.7×, where the
  note recorded 3.77 vs 3.69 against us. That claim is cited in `ROADMAP.md` and in
  §17, and it is the sole counterexample either carries to S6.
- **The "<2% run-to-run spread" claim does not hold for `agg`.** Twelve consecutive
  runs of one binary on `agg_50000` span 1.466–2.357 s, a **61% spread**, while
  `chain_400` and `sparse_400` sit at 4%. It is not thermal (38 °C, no trend across
  runs) and not core placement (`taskset -c 0` does not change it); the excursions
  hit every build equally, stock and cut alike. **`min`-of-N is the estimator to
  use**, and best-of-3 already is `min`-of-3, which is why the sweep numbers held up.
  The excursions do not explain a 2.9× gap on their own.

Nothing here impugns the cross-engine note's *structural* findings — both engines
agreeing byte-for-byte, the front-end/evaluation split, and semi-naive-vs-naive as
an exponent all stand. What does not survive is the wall clock at the top end.

## Where this leaves the recorder decision — read this before deciding it

Measuring the recorder against **today's** engine gives an answer that is wrong by
an order of magnitude, in the direction that lets it off the hook. Cut RB is cut R
and cut B together — the recorder's share of a post-seek engine:

| shape | recorder's share today | after cut R |
|---|---|---|
| sparse_800 | 2% | **55%** |
| sparse_400 | 24% | **60%** |
| chain_800 | 17% | 50% |
| negation_400 | 23% | 55% |
| agg_50000 | 12% | 4% |

The scan is hiding the recorder. Remove the scan and provenance becomes **half the
run** on every recursive shape, on top of the 78% of memory it already costs.

So the ROADMAP's sequencing note — *"whichever is settled first constrains the
other"* — is right, and this profile adds a third term: **the recorder question has
to be decided against the post-seek engine, not against this one.** Deciding it
today would price pillar 1 at 2% of a run that no longer exists.

## What this does not answer

- **The sampling is shallow** — self-time only, on four programs, from a profiler
  added late in the session. It confirmed and re-ranked leads that were already
  named; it was not run broadly enough to *discover* one.
- **Cut R is unreviewed**, and does not touch the negated-atom or aggregate-goal
  arms, which still scan. Its interaction with join-order selection is unexamined —
  seeking makes body order matter *more*, not less.
- **No imports.** The corpus is inline facts, so §13's path is still measured only by
  `performance-baseline.md`, whose fact base no longer exists.
- **One machine, synthetic graphs**, and — on the evidence above — a machine whose
  wall clock is less trustworthy than three files have assumed.
- **`--no-default-features` does not pass its own test suite**: `system.rs`'s two
  temporal tests fail there on stock, because they import date columns. Pre-existing,
  unrelated to anything here, and a supported configuration (§17 2026-07-23).

**Do not optimise from this file** beyond cut R, which is measured, guarded and
green. The rest of it says where the time *is not*.
